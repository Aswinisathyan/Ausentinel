About Ausentinel

Ausentinel is a research prototype that detects spoofed speech: recordings made by text-to-speech or voice conversion instead of a real person.

How it works

  1. Prepare the audio. The recording is converted to one channel at 16,000 samples per second, and cut or padded to exactly {{ max_duration|int }} seconds.
  2. Describe the sound. The audio is split into short overlapping slices. For each slice, the energy in 20 equally spaced frequency bands is measured and compressed into 20 numbers called LFCCs (linear frequency cepstral coefficients).
  3. Summarise. The averages, spreads and changes over time of those numbers give 80 features for the whole clip.
  4. Score. A support vector machine (SVM), trained on thousands of real and fake recordings, turns the 80 features into one score. Higher means more spoof-like.
  5. Decide. Scores at or above {{ "%.4f"|format(threshold) }} are called spoof.

Reading your result

Score
Where the recording falls relative to the model's boundary between real and fake. It is not a probability, so the app does not show a confidence percentage.
Threshold ({{ "%.4f"|format(threshold) }})
The cut-off point. It was chosen on development data so that the share of spoofs missed and the share of real voices flagged were about equal (around 7.2% each).
Borderline (within ±{{ borderline_margin }})
About 1 in 5 development recordings scored this close to the threshold. For them, balanced accuracy was 68.8%, against 97.9% for the rest. Treat borderline results with extra caution.

Files and privacy

  • Accepted formats: {{ allowed }}, up to {{ max_upload_mb }} MB.
  • Only the first {{ max_duration|int }} seconds are analysed.
  • MP3 is not accepted. In testing, MP3 compression made all 30 real recordings in a sample look fake, because it removes high-frequency sound that this model depends on. M4A had no such effect.
  • Uploaded files are deleted as soon as they have been scored. They are never stored.

Limitations

  • On attack methods it never saw in training, the model misses about a third of spoofs. See the results.
  • It was trained on clean studio recordings. Phone calls, background noise and other audio processing have not been tested.
  • The 2019 attack methods are older than many voice-cloning tools available today.
  • It is a research prototype. Do not use it as the only basis for a security decision.

Technology

Python, librosa and SciPy for audio processing; scikit-learn for the StandardScaler and SVM with an RBF kernel; Flask and Waitress for the web app; ffmpeg for M4A conversion.

Trained and evaluated on the ASVspoof 2019 Logical Access database. See the dataset's licence file for its terms of use.
